ComfyUI   AIprompting does not need to be complicated.

You don’t need 20 tags, a secret formula, or a perfect prompt structure. A good prompt simply gives ComfyUI a clear idea of the genre, mood, vocals, instruments, production, and tempo you want.

A useful starting formula is:

Genre + Mood + Vocals + Instruments + Production + Tempo

For example:

dark alternative R&B, emotional male vocals, Rhodes piano,
deep bass, sparse drums, warm analog production, 88 BPM

This guide explains what is actually useful, what is mostly community advice, and how to troubleshoot common ComfyUI results.

1. How to Write a Good ComfyUI Prompt

Start with the most important musical information.

A simple prompt can include:

    Genre: pop, rock, R&B, hip-hop, jazz, EDM
    Mood: emotional, dark, joyful, nostalgic, aggressive
    Vocals: male, female, breathy, raspy, powerful, intimate
    Instruments: piano, guitar, strings, synths, drums
    Production: warm, cinematic, lo-fi, dry, live, modern
    Tempo: slow, mid-tempo, 90 BPM, 128 BPM

For example:

cinematic pop, emotional, powerful female vocals,
piano and orchestral strings, soft drums,
wide modern production, 100 BPM

You can use fewer elements when the song is simple.

The goal is clarity, not maximum detail.

There is no official ComfyUI rule that says you must use exactly five, eight, or ten tags. Those numbers come from community experimentation.

If a prompt becomes difficult to read, it is probably doing too much.
2. Put Style Instructions in the Style Field

Use the Style field for the overall sound of the song.

For example:

Bollywood romantic pop, emotional female vocals,
sitar, tabla, lush strings, warm cinematic production, 110 BPM

Use the Lyrics field for your lyrics and song structure.

For example:

[Verse 1]

I still remember your voice
Calling my name in the rain
[Pre-Chorus]
Maybe we were never meant
To let this go
[Chorus]
Come back to me
Before the silence wins
I still need you here

Keeping these two jobs separate makes the workflow easier to control.

Don’t treat [Verse], [Chorus], or [Bridge] like programming commands. They are musical directions, and ComfyUI may interpret them differently between generations.
3. Useful Song Structure Tags

Common section labels include:

[Intro]
[Verse 1]
[Pre-Chorus]
[Chorus]
[Verse 2]
[Bridge]
[Breakdown]
[Instrumental]
[Outro]

You can also add a short performance direction:

[Verse 1]

(whispered)
I can hear your footsteps
Outside my door

Or:

[Chorus]

(powerful, emotional)
I won't let you go
I won't let you disappear

Performance cues can help communicate your intention, but they are not guaranteed commands.

powerful emotional vocal delivery

Experiment with both.
4. Don’t Believe Every “ComfyUI Prompt Rule”

👉 Want a larger library of ready-to-use prompts? I’ve compiled 10,000+ tested ComfyUI   AIprompts with copy-paste formulas organized across 200 genres.

A lot of ComfyUI advice online is presented as fact when it is really a creator experiment.

For example, you may hear:

    Genre must be the first tag.
    You must use exactly eight tags.
    “Instrumental” must be the final word.
    ComfyUI reads every tag from left to right with a fixed weighting system.
    More than 10 tags automatically makes a prompt worse.

There is no official ComfyUI documentation establishing these as universal rules.

That doesn’t mean the techniques are useless.

Putting the main genre early is sensible because it makes your intention clear.

Keeping the prompt short is sensible because fewer conflicting instructions are easier to manage.

Putting instrumental clearly in the prompt makes your intention obvious.

Just don’t confuse useful prompting habits with hard platform specifications.
5. How to Use BPM

You can include a BPM target:

88 BPM

or:

slow tempo, around 72 BPM

BPM should be treated as a musical target rather than assuming ComfyUI will behave like a DAW with a locked metronome.

If the exact tempo matters, check the generated track.

For example:

dark synthwave, driving male vocals,
analog synths, punchy drums, cinematic production, 110 BPM

is a useful starting point.
6. How to Get Better Vocals

“Male vocals” or “female vocals” is often not enough.

Describe the character you want.

Try:

deep male baritone, slightly raspy, intimate delivery

or:

soft breathy female vocals, emotional and intimate

or:

powerful female vocals, clear high notes, dramatic delivery

You can also describe the recording:

close-miked vocals, dry mix, natural dynamics

If the vocal sounds too processed, don’t immediately rewrite the entire prompt.

Change one thing.

For example:

breathy male vocals

could become:

breathy male vocals, warm and natural, close-miked

Then generate again.
7. How to Make   AIMusic Sound Less Artificial

If your generation sounds overly polished or sterile, try describing the performance and production you want.

Useful phrases include:

live performance feel
natural dynamics
human timing
close-miked recording
dry intimate mix
analog warmth
tape saturation

For example:

indie rock, emotional male vocals,
live drums, electric guitar, bass,
natural dynamics, slight tape saturation,
live performance feel

These phrases don’t “remove AI.” They simply tell ComfyUI to aim for a less sterile musical character.

9. What to Do When ComfyUI Gives the Wrong Result

Don’t change everything at once.

Find the biggest problem and change one part of the prompt.
Wrong genre

Simplify the genre description.

Instead of:

pop, rock, jazz, EDM, trap, folk

try:

alternative pop, emotional female vocals,
electric guitar, live drums, warm modern production

Too many instruments

Try:

sparse arrangement, piano and bass, light percussion

Weak chorus

Make the chorus shorter and more direct.

[Chorus]

Don't let me go
Don't let me disappear
I need you here

Weak vocals

Try:

powerful emotional female vocal,
strong chorus delivery

Too much reverb

Try:

dry mix, close-miked vocals, minimal room ambience

Generic production

Add one or two specific characteristics:

tape saturation, muted trumpet, dry close-miked vocals

The important rule is:

Fix one problem at a time.
